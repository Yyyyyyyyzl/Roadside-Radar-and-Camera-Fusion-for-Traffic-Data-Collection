# Roadside-Radar-and-Camera-Fusion-for-Traffic-Data-Collection
This repository documents and describes the dataset used in GMDiff: Graph-Motif Matrix Enhanced Conditional Diffusion Model for Vehicle Trajectory Generation, including a README file and data examples.

The nuScenes dataset is a public multimodal autonomous driving dataset collected in Boston and Singapore, containing 1,000 manually curated real-world driving scenes, each lasting 20 seconds, with approximately 15 hours of total recording duration. It adopts a comprehensive sensor suite consisting of 1 LiDAR, 5 radars, 6 RGB cameras, IMU and GPS, and provides 3D annotations covering 23 object categories at a keyframe sampling rate of 2 Hz, including around 1.4 million camera images, 390,000 LiDAR sweeps, 1.4 million radar sweeps and 1.4 million 3D bounding boxes. For the trajectory prediction benchmark, it supports the standard setting of using 2 seconds of historical motion to predict future 6-second trajectories, yielding more than 49,000 valid 8-second trajectory sequences. For our experiments, 6,000 trajectory sequences were selected from nuScenes after preprocessing.

The second self-collected dataset is constructed from radar-camera fusion tracking trajectories acquired at a high-density multi-phase urban intersection. Continuous data output has been ongoing since July 2025. The subset adopted in this paper is sampled from one month of continuous observation, including 1,000 independent 20-second scenes, which is consistent with the scene-level granularity of nuScenes. The dataset covers typical intersection behaviors such as straight-going, left-turning, right-turning and U-turns under mixed traffic flow. It contains a total of 3000 unique vehicle trajectories, with additional annotations for non-vehicle participants including pedestrians and two-wheelers. Raw fusion tracks are sampled at 10 Hz, and the prediction task follows the identical input-output configuration as nuScenes: 2 seconds of historical observations are used to predict the subsequent 6-second future motion, forming 8-second trajectory samples. Geographic WGS84 coordinates of all agents are converted to a local metric east-north coordinate system with the intersection center as the origin before model training and evaluation; lane topology, lane turning attributes and stop-line information are also stored as map priors. The 6,000 processed trajectory sequences used in this study are extracted from this dataset.

For each dataset, the 6,000 processed trajectory sequences were divided into training, validation, and test sets in a 70%/10%/20% ratio. This corresponds to 4,200 training sequences, 600 validation sequences, and 1,200 test sequences for each dataset. The split is performed at the scene level after trajectory preprocessing to avoid temporal leakage between training and test samples.
